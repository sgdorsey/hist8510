Alabama

The program in Alabama is the second oldest in the
length of time organized. The program has had the time to gain
a substantial place in the life of the people of the state.
There is a state-wide committee of laymen insterested in promoting
a state-wide program on a permanent basis. The program func-
tions in every section of the state, in all of the municipalities,
and in many rural areas. The training program has been extensive
in nature and a number of people have gone from the WPA program
into permanent recreational employment.

A statistical paragraph as of February, 1937 indicates
the extent of the program. A narrative picture of the activities
within some of the counties for that same month give a colorful
account of the extensiveness of the program.

Narrative Report of Recreation
Program

Blount County:

The recreation work over the county is progressing very
nicely with all centers doing very good work. Basketball is the
main activity or has been for the month of February, but now more
interest is being shown in kite-flying, badminton and volley
ball. The Washington's birthday parties and dance were the main
events of the month. Everyone enjoyed them and the Valentine
parties at the various centers. The staff meetings and training
courses twice monthly have proven interesting and instructive.
Wood-work in the handicraft classes is coming to the front very
fast as everybody is intensely interested in this activity.

Clay County:

     The feature events in the life of recreation for the month were as
follows:  (1) the establishing of a work shop in which recreation
equipment can be made and repaired; (2) an inspirational training
course which created a new attitude among the personnel; and (3) the
Community Night party which was given at the center.

Colbert County:

     In addition to the regular daily playground activities some very
interesting special programs have been presented.  At both of the
colored centers and at Valdosta effective Valentine programs and
parties were held.  At Valdosta 15 little girls took part in a play,
"Columbine and Pierette."  The costumes for this play were made by
the mothers at the play center.  Washington's Birthday was celebrated
with most effective program at the Mothers' Circle Center.

     A Colonial Party was conducted by one of the Recreation leaders
for the Tuscumbia Mothers' Club February 22.  This was their annual
social function.  In addition to this party, the same leader con-
ducted a social for the Y. W. A. of the Baptist Church, a social for
the Young People's Department of the Methodist Church, a banquet
for the Adult Department of the Baptist Church, and a picnic for the
B. T. U. of the Baptist Church.  New interest in folk dancing and
girls' activities have been shown at the Tuscumbia Center.

Etowah County:

     Work on the five playgrounds continued as usual.  An inter-school
marble tournement was played off, and the winner officially announced.

     Classes and Clubs have recently been reorganized at the Cottage
to reach all age groups..

     The stoory hour for small children on Monday afternoon, a woodcraft
class for boys on Tuesday, a handicraft class for ladies on Wednesday,
a handicraft class for girls on Thursday, folk dancing Friday, and
hiking on Saturday, are some of the features on the program.

     Two nights each week the Cottage is open for supervised play.  On
Music Night an appropriate party is usually featured.  An "Honest Abe"
party and a George Washington party have been given.

     Folk dancing has attracted such a large crowd that it has been
necessary to locate another auditorium large enough to accommodate
the crowd.  Interest is growing in quiet games which are featured
on Wednesday nights.

     The Pre-Play Center had handicraft suitable for their age--making
of valentines, cherries, hatchets, and Mother Goose silhouettes and

Lauderdale County:

  One hundred eight adults attended and took part in the February
Community program at Killen. This group is showing a growing interest
in recreational activities and is anxious for a play center for their
own. Information on the boys' countywide Marble Tournament has been
sent out, and A clever set of marionettes depicting the story of
Rumpelstiltskin has been constructed on the project. These have
attracted quite a lot of favorable notice and numerous invitations
for performances. Added interest in the Negro Chorus has come through
invitations to sing at sever 1 local meetings. The men's volley ball
league is playing three nights a week, and the girls' basketball
two nights. Both groups are at high pitch and drawing good crowds.
Play groups and community gatherings have functioned as usual.

Mobile County:

  Bicycle parades, balloon parades, treasure hunts, and amateur
contests were held on both white and colored playgrounds during the
first week of the month. Several of the safety patrol groups were
taken on hikes during the month by the recreation workers.


                         Colored

  There have been six socials held at night during the month, and
the music for them was furnished by the colored WPA recreation band.

  Three local community council meetings have been held at Plateau,
Prichard, and Harmon. The councils are working on activities that
will give them something to put in the treasury to help buy equipment.

  On Saturday, February 27, a very interesting talk was given to
the Safety Patrol boys by a member of the traffic department of the
city. Workers have been placed at the Colored Old Folks' Home and
at the Association for the Blind. The workers visit these places
twice a week and read, tell stories, and discuss topics of interest
to the inmates.

Montgomery County:

  In addition to the regular activities on the various playgrounds
throughout the city, on February 1 a Negro Community Sing was held at
the State Teachers' College with an attendance of 150 people. The
NYA workers throughout the city were entertained at a George Washington
Dance on February 22. The dance was sponsored by the Recreation Project
and more than 125 persons attended.

  Volley ball courts have been completed at Kiwanis and Hamner Hall
parks for white children, while three volley ball courts have been
dompleted on the grounds of Booker Washington, Hale, and Loveless
Schools for the Negroes. A basketball court is under construction
at Oak Park.

                In White County a county-wide basis play night on
April 1st was well attended. The Rotary Club is sponsoring a
new recreational project in Woodruff County.
    When the new Division was created, the Assistant Director in charge
of Recreation set up as rapidly as possible a state-wide organization
conforming to the WPA set-up in the state. A junior glee club was organized,
ball tournament was held in which forty-six people participated.
    Realizing the necessity for closer correlation between Community

Life and the Recreation work, more than 150 Recreation Councils
composed of more than 1,000 public-spirited citizens who are giving
their time unselfishly to promote the Recreation program in their
various communities, have been established. The Recreation workers

in practically every instance have worked in the closest harmony
with the public school authorities, with civic, fraternal, and
religious organizations. In Miller County the Kiwanis Club furnished the eggs
at the Municipal Auditorium to raise funds for equipment.

                          March, 1937

District No. 1 - The most important event of the month in the first
district was a radio broadcast from Station KBTM at Jonesboro,
explaining the recreation program. In Craighead County the Young
Men's Club is sponsoring a recreational park and all civic organizations
are cooperating in some phase of the program.

    The PTA is rendering valuable assistance in Mississippi County.
Five playgrounds will be in operation in Blytheville during the summer
months under volunteer workers furnished by this organizations. Another
volunteer worker is conducting a class in boxing. About thirty boys
are participating, and bouts are held on each Monday evening. In Osceola
there are four playgrounds that are being supervised by volunteer workers.

    In Batesville the people are taking a great deal of interest in
street contests, especially skating. The mayor permits certain streets
to be blocked off on three nights each week.

    In White County a county-wide basis play night on
April 1st was well attended. The Rotary Club is sponsoring a
new recreational project in Woodruff County.

<u>District No. 2</u> - March was a busy month throughout the second
district. In Lafayette County the council presented a minstrel
which was well attended. A Junior glee club was organized, a
three act drama was presented by the Dramatic Club. A basket
ball tournament was held in which forty-six people participated.
The recreation study groups are meeting weekly.

    In Columbia County the Boys are taking great interest
in handicraft. Several skating parties have been held and band
concerts have been arranged for the Summer. A sunrise break-
fast hike and picnics have been greatly enjoyed by the younger
groups. A kite tournament and Easter egg hunt attracted large
crowds. In Sevier County the community hall is the center of
activities. The demand is so great that the hall is being kept
open at night.

    In Miller County the Kiwanis Club furnished the eggs
for the Easter egg hunt. The colored people are giving a play
at the Municipal Auditorium to raise funds for equipment.
Several soft ball teams have been organized and are playing
regularly. In Dallas and Ouachita Counties, volleyball,
softball, ping-pong, J-Ball, Badminton, cards and dominoes
are providing proving most popular at this time. Kite flying
contests and community singing are attracting good crowds.

    The Council sponsored a dance at Kingsland which
netted about eighty-five dollars for equipment. Volleyball,
softball, horseshoe pitching and washer pitching are proving
popular. The Negro Dramatic Club presented a program at the
Baptist church from which forty-five dollars were realized for
equipment.

    An old-fashioned spelling bee and basketball games
that were held at the high school gymnasium were well attended
at Hope. In Bradley County and Calhoun County kite flying
contests and athletic contests attracted large crowds. Soft-
ball and basketball teams have been organized and plans are
underway for a field day to be held late in the Spring.

     Field and track meets and May festivals stressing
health are among the activities now being planned for April and
May. Councils and leaders are already arranging for play areas
and equipment for summer playgrounds, with increased space for
tennis courts; hunting and fishing and swimming facilities.
Activities in each community will center around groups and
individual interests already manifest in the locality and deter-
mined largely by the facilities at hand. Several new activities
such as archery and choral clubs will be introduced where there
is a popular demand.

     Hot Springs recreational leaders are forming a county
study group, to take a course in the "Theory and Practice of
Play", and similar training courses are contemplated in Greater
Little Rock, Pulaski County.

     Leaders are being urged to read recreation literature,
and make use of bulletins where available. Through Sunday
Schools, scouting, YMCA's and YWCA's, 4-H Clubs and similar
groups, in Pulaski County, Jefferson County, Little Rock and
N. Little Rock recreation projects are extending the service
to Negro citizens as well as to the white population.

<u>District</u> <u>No.</u> <u>4</u> - Play days were held in the various communities
in Benton County. Playground equipment has been provided in a
number of communities for the children. The principal activity

provided eggs for an Easter egg hunt at Siloam Springs. In
Washington County most of the work has been with the youngsters
in school, 4-H Clubs, Home Demonstration Clubs, social recrea-
tion nights and play days. Softball teams and archery teams
have been organized. Prairie Grove has provided a community
center and has agreed to pay the operating expense. It is
planned to have community meetings for adults at least three

     In Johnson and Pope Counties plans are underway for
county track meets open to boys, girls, men and women. Archery
is proving quite popular among both children and dults.
Amateur nights have been well attended and people are cooperating
more fully each week. Yell County's greater portion of activities
have been devoted to drama, archery, community singing, and
handicraft as well as the usual routine among the various play-
grounds. More interest is being manifested among adults, due to
an active recreational council. Regular community nights are
being conducted with attendance growing each time.

                                      Summary

                Recreation Councils:  With the appointment of 28
          additional councils since the months of January makes a total
          of 148 Councils, with a total of 1953 members on the councils.
          The first state meeting of recreation councils was held in
          Little Rock, April 4.  Fifteen counties were represented with
          over seventy-five council members present.  These council
          members brought over seventy supervisors to the meeting without
          any expense to the Federal Government.  Several council mem-
          bers took an active part in the discussion and some suggested
          that another meeting be held within six months.  The Regional
          Director was present.

                Basketball:  The State Independent Basketball Tourna-
          ment was held in Little Rock, March 20.  Four District winners
          took part.  Clinton, from District No. 1 won first place.  Over
          two hundred basketball teams took part in the basketball program.
          A basketball goal shooting contest was ehel held with a total of
          4,263 contestants.  District No. 2, Pine Bluff won most of the
          events.
